EASTER EGG 02
"PER ARENAM AD ASTRA", IN MORSE CODE, UNDER THE DIE
======================================================================

WHAT
  A strip of 36 bars in two widths, spelling out in International Morse:

      PER ARENAM AD ASTRA

  Latin, roughly "through the sand, to the stars", playing on per aspera
  ad astra. Sand is silicon and the puzzle is about stars, so it works
  twice.

WHERE
  puzzle/puzzle.gds
    layer 200/0, which is not a sky130 mask layer at all
    all 36 bars at y = -52.72 um, spanning x = 1.33 .. 198.67 um
    below the die: the placement area starts at y = 0, so nothing here
    would ever be fabricated

  The bars are placements of two structures that are neither standard cells
  nor vias:

      INTERNAL_3   1.38 um wide x 2.72 um tall   21 placements   the dot
      INTERNAL_7   4.14 um wide x 2.72 um tall   15 placements   the dash

  4.14 / 1.38 = 3 exactly.

HOW
  This is the one I am happiest about, because I found it without knowing
  there was anything to find. It came straight out of the GDS inventory,
  which is the first thing my pipeline builds, and three separate lines of
  that inventory pointed at the same place.

  1. The top cell's bounding box came back as

         (0.00, -52.72) .. (200.00, 300.00)

     A negative y. The placement rows of a standard cell design start at
     y = 0, so there is something drawn 52.72 um below where the chip is.

  2. The placement histogram splits into logic cells, vias, taps, decaps,
     antenna diodes, and a last bucket for anything that is none of those.
     That bucket was not empty:

         21 x INTERNAL_3
         15 x INTERNAL_7

     36 placements of two structures with no pins, no transistors and names
     no PDK uses.

  3. The layer histogram lists every (layer, datatype) pair in the file
     against the sky130 layer map, and flags the ones the map does not
     know. Layer 200/0 is flagged. It carries 2 polygons: one inside each
     of those two structures.

  Then the confirming step, which is the one that made it a message rather
  than a curiosity. Both the puzzle and the warm-up went through the same
  flow, so any layer present in one and absent from the other was put there
  on purpose. That diff gives three entries and separates cleanly:

      66/15    only inside standard cells  -> a cell-library difference
      81/23    only inside standard cells  -> a cell-library difference
      200/0    outside every standard cell -> hand added

  66/15 and 81/23 appear only because the puzzle instantiates conb_1 and
  diode_2 and the warm-up has neither. 200/0 is the only genuinely
  hand-added layer in the file. Nothing else in either design is decoration.

  With 36 bars in two widths at a 1:3 ratio, the thing to measure is the
  gaps. Dividing them by the narrow bar width, every gap is exactly 1, 3
  or 7 units:

    widths  : . - - . . . - . . - . - . . - . . - - - . - - . . . - . . . - . - . . -
    gaps    : 1 1 1 3 3 1 1 7 1 3 1 1 3 3 1 3 1 3 1 7 1 3 1 1 7 1 3 1 1 3 3 1 1 3 1

  Short mark, long mark at 3, gaps at 1 inside a letter, 3 between letters,
  7 between words. That is International Morse timing exactly, and it
  decodes with no ambiguity:

    P    .--.
    E    .
    R    .-.
         (word gap)
    A    .-
    R    .-.
    E    .
    N    -.
    A    .-
    M    --
         (word gap)
    A    .-
    D    -..
         (word gap)
    A    .-
    S    ...
    T    -
    R    .-.
    A    .-

  4 + 1 + 3 + 2 + 3 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 3 + 2 + 3 + 1 + 3 + 2 = 36 marks.
  Every bar is accounted for.

  The instance counts settle which cell is which without measuring
  anything: the decode needs exactly 21 dots and 15 dashes, INTERNAL_3 is
  placed 21 times and INTERNAL_7 is placed 15 times. So INTERNAL_3 is the
  dot and INTERNAL_7 is the dash, which the widths then confirm. The 3 and
  the 7 in the names are the letter gap and the word gap, the two numbers
  you need to know to read the strip at all.

WHEN
  Day 1, within an hour of the logo, as soon as the inventory script
  printed a negative bounding box and two structures it could not classify.

  No script in this repository decodes it any more, because it does not
  need to. The pipeline's inventory stage still prints the negative
  bounding box, the two unclassified structures and the unknown layer, and
  from there the decode is done by hand once. It is in
  puzzle-solution/01_gds_inventory.txt.
