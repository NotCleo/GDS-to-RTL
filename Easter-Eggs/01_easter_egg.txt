EASTER EGG 01
THE JANE STREET LOGO, ETCHED IN METAL 2
======================================================================

WHAT
  The Jane Street mark, drawn as real mask geometry on the second metal
  layer. 1,366 sub-micron polygons packed into a 17.10 x 17.10 um square.
  It is electrically floating: it connects to nothing, carries no signal,
  and my extractor sees it as 1,366 shapes that merge into islands no pin
  ever lands on.

WHERE
  puzzle/puzzle.gds
    layer 69/20, which is met2 drawing
    x 34.90 .. 52.00 um, y 35.20 .. 52.30 um

  It is in the warm-up GDS too, same 1,366 polygons, same 17.10 um square:
  warmup/04_final.gds at x 65.90 .. 83.00, y 66.20 .. 83.30.

HOW
  The first find, and it came from looking rather than from code. Before
  writing anything I opened puzzle.gds in the Tiny Tapeout browser viewer
  and then in GDS3D, and turned layers off one at a time to see what each
  one was doing. On met2 there is a pale ring low on the die that does not
  look like anything else in the file. Routing on a metal layer is long
  power straps and short jogs between vias. This was neither: hundreds of
  tiny squares crammed into one small block, in a place with no cells under
  it.

  Zooming in, it resolved into the concentric-circle mark. Rasterising the
  polygon bounding boxes to ASCII later confirmed it:

  The question that makes it findable without knowing it is there: which
  layer has hundreds of very small polygons crowded into one small square?
  At the top level of a routed design, nothing else looks like that, and
  exactly one layer answers.

WHEN
  Day 1, first hour, in the GDS viewer, before I had written a line of
  code. It is also visible in the warm-up, so anyone doing the warm-up
  properly can find this one on day one without touching the puzzle.
