EASTER EGG 03
"LEAVE NO STONE UNTURNED!" IN THE WAVEFORM HEADER
======================================================================

WHAT
  A note left for a human in the $version field of the sample waveform:

      Leave no stone unturned! But for this file, consider looking at it
      in a waveform viewer instead.

  No simulator writes a $version field like that. iverilog writes
  "Icarus Verilog", Verilator writes its own banner. This is a person
  talking to you, and it is telling you two things: that there is more in
  this file than the obvious, and where to go and look for it.

WHERE
  puzzle/example_inputs.vcd, lines 4 to 6, the $version block:

      $date
        Sat Dec 31 23:59:60 2016
      $end
      $version
        Leave no stone unturned! But for this file, consider looking at it
        in a waveform viewer instead.
      $end
      $timescale
        1ps
      $end

HOW
  By opening the file in a text editor.

  That sounds too obvious to write down, so here is the honest version. A
  VCD is a waveform, so my instinct was to treat it as one: drag it into
  Surfer, or feed it to a parser. I did both. What I did not do at first
  was read it as text, which is what it actually is, and the line is inside
  the first ten lines of the file.

  I got there by deciding to be systematic rather than clever. There are
  only three puzzle files, so I gave each one a first pass that assumed
  nothing:

      puzzle.gds            open in a viewer, then inventory it
      layout.png            read the labels, note the ports
      example_inputs.vcd    open it raw, then open it in Surfer

  The raw pass on the VCD is what found this, and eggs 04 and 06 with it.
  It also cost nothing: the header is nine lines long.

  For completeness, all three files also went through exiftool. Nothing.
  layout.png is a plain 880 x 1000 RGBA PNG with no comment, author or
  custom chunk, and the other two are not formats exiftool has anything to
  say about. That negative result is worth recording because it is the
  obvious first thing to try, and it is a dead end. The interesting metadata
  in this puzzle is inside the file formats, not attached to them.

WHEN
  Day 1, during the first pass over the three provided files, a few minutes
  after the GDS viewer finds. It is quoted verbatim in
  puzzle-solution/04_vcd_replay.txt, because the pipeline prints both
  header fields as part of validating the extraction against this file.
