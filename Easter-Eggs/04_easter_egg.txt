EASTER EGG 04
A TIMESTAMP THAT MOST CLOCKS NEVER SHOWED
======================================================================

WHAT
  The $date field of the sample waveform reads:

      Sat Dec 31 23:59:60 2016

  A second numbered 60. On the face of it that is a broken timestamp, and
  most date parsers will reject it, because most date libraries assume a
  minute has sixty seconds numbered 0 to 59.

  It is not broken. It is a leap second, and a real one. 31 December 2016
  at 23:59:60 UTC is the most recent leap second inserted into UTC: the
  IERS added one that night, so that minute had 61 seconds. The weekday is
  correct as well, it was a Saturday.

WHERE
  puzzle/example_inputs.vcd, lines 1 to 3, immediately above egg 03:

      $date
        Sat Dec 31 23:59:60 2016
      $end

HOW
  Same moment as egg 03, two lines above it. Once you have decided to open
  the VCD in a text editor instead of only in a waveform viewer, both are
  free.

  I noticed it because I had just read the $version line, so I was reading
  the header carefully instead of skipping to $enddefinitions. The :60
  stands out if you read the characters rather than the shape of a date.

  Checking it was one search: leap seconds are a short list, 27 of them
  since 1972, and 2016-12-31 is the last entry on it.

WHEN
  Day 1, the same minute as egg 03. The pipeline prints it every run, in
  puzzle-solution/04_vcd_replay.txt, next to the replay result, because
  both facts describe the same file: it is a recording of a real chip, and
  it is dated with a second most software rejects.
