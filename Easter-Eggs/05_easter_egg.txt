EASTER EGG 05
THE BLOG POST TELLS YOU TO READ THE WAVEFORM AS ASCII
======================================================================

WHAT
  Not hidden in a file: hidden in plain sight in the puzzle announcement.

  The blog post that sets the puzzle links, in passing, to another Jane
  Street post about using ASCII waveforms to test hardware designs. Read as
  a reading recommendation it is a curiosity. Read as an instruction it is
  the key to the whole output side of the puzzle: the eight-bit bus O[7:0]
  is not a number, it is a character.

      https://blog.janestreet.com/can-you-reverse-engineer-an-asic/
      https://blog.janestreet.com/using-ascii-waveforms-to-test-hardware-designs/

  Screenshot of the moment I noticed: Images/switching-to-ascii-moment.png

WHERE
  The puzzle blog post, in the body text, near the mention of the sample
  waveform. Not in any of the provided files.

HOW
  I had the sample waveform open in Surfer and had got exactly nothing out
  of it. success is low for the whole trace. O[7:0] does something: it sits
  at zero, then changes several times in a burst near the end, then goes
  back to zero. Displayed as unsigned decimal or as hex, that burst is:

      hex        54 52 59 20 41 47 41 49 4e
      decimal    84 82 89 32 65 71 65 73 78

  which, as numbers, says nothing at all. I stared at it as decimal, as hex
  and as binary and moved on, assuming the output block was the part the
  hint image told me I could ignore.

  Then I went back to the blog post to re-read the rules, and the ASCII
  waveform link was sitting there. I do almost all my waveform reading in
  hex or unsigned, so switching a bus to ASCII is not a habit I have. In
  Surfer it is a right-click on the signal, Format, ASCII.

  The same nine bytes immediately read:

      T  R  Y     A  G  A  I  N

  Screenshot: Images/try_again_message.png, at the 1255000 ps marker.

  That reframed the entire puzzle in one step. The chip is not returning a
  status code, it is talking. The block the hint image labels "output
  generator, safe to ignore at first" is a ROM of English sentences. And
  the sample waveform is not a neutral demonstration, it is the chip
  telling whoever recorded it that they got it wrong. Which means there is
  something to get right, and the chip will say so.

  It also told me where to point the rest of the work: if O is ASCII, then
  seven bits is a character, and that is what unlocked egg 06 in the
  opposite direction, on the input side.

WHEN
  Day 1, after the first pass over the files, while re-reading the puzzle
  statement. This is the one on this list that actually changed how I
  attacked the problem, which is why I count it as an easter egg rather
  than as a hint I missed.
