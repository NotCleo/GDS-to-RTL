EASTER EGG 05
THE BLOG POST TELLS YOU TO READ THE WAVEFORM AS ASCII
======================================================================

WHAT
  Not in any provided file. It is in the puzzle announcement.

  The blog post that sets the puzzle links, in passing, to another Jane
  Street post about using ASCII waveforms to test hardware designs. Read as
  a recommendation it is a curiosity. Read as an instruction it is what
  makes the output side readable: the eight-bit bus O[7:0] is not a number,
  it is a character.

      https://blog.janestreet.com/can-you-reverse-engineer-an-asic/
      https://blog.janestreet.com/using-ascii-waveforms-to-test-hardware-designs/

  Screenshot of the moment I noticed: Images/switching-to-ascii-moment.png

WHERE
  The puzzle blog post, in the body text, near the mention of the sample
  waveform. Not in any of the provided files.

HOW
  I had the sample waveform open in Surfer and had got exactly nothing out
  of it. success is low for the whole trace. O[7:0] does something: it sits
  at zero, then changes several times in a burst near the end, then goes
  back to zero. Displayed as unsigned decimal or as hex, that burst is:

      hex        54 52 59 20 41 47 41 49 4e
      decimal    84 82 89 32 65 71 65 73 78

  which, as numbers, says nothing. I read it as decimal, as hex and as
  binary, then moved on, assuming the output block was the part the hint
  image said I could ignore.

  Then I went back to the blog post to re-read the rules, and the ASCII
  waveform link was there. I do almost all my waveform reading in hex or
  unsigned, so switching a bus to ASCII is not a habit I have. In Surfer it
  is a right-click on the signal, Format, ASCII.

  The same nine bytes immediately read:

      T  R  Y     A  G  A  I  N

  Screenshot: Images/try_again_message.png, at the 1255000 ps marker.

  So the chip does not return a status code, it returns text. The block the
  hint image labels "output generator, safe to ignore at first" is a ROM of
  English sentences, and the sample waveform is a recording of a wrong grid
  being rejected.

  It also pointed at the input side: if O is ASCII, then seven bits is a
  character, which is what led to egg 06.

WHEN
  Day 1, after the first pass over the files, while re-reading the puzzle
  statement. It is listed as an easter egg rather than as a missed hint
  because it changed the approach to the output side entirely.
