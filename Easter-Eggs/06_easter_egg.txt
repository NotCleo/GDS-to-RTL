EASTER EGG 06
"THE NIGHT SKY AWAITS", HIDDEN IN THE SAMPLE INPUTS
======================================================================

WHAT
  The sample waveform is described in the puzzle as "some inputs being fed
  to the design (unfortunately, not the correct inputs to make success go
  high!)". For most of day one I read that as a failed solve attempt and
  treated the file as scenery.

  It is not a failed attempt. The inputs are a message. Read the 121 bits
  of each frame as an 11 x 11 grid, take each row as a 7-bit ASCII
  character with column 0 as the least significant bit, and the two frames
  say:

      "The night s" + "ky awaits  "  =  "The night sky awaits"

WHERE
  puzzle/example_inputs.vcd, the I input, sampled on every rising clock
  edge while enable is high. There are two frames of 121 bits, separated by
  a reset.

HOW
  Egg 05 had just taught me that eight bits on this chip means a character.
  That put the same question on the input side: what if I is carrying text
  too?

  Two things gave the payload away before I decoded anything, and both come
  from just counting.

  1. Both frames contain exactly 38 stars. Not similar, identical. A random
     wrong guess and a second random wrong guess do not agree to the bit on
     their star count.

  2. In both frames, columns 7, 8, 9 and 10 are empty in every single row.
     44 of the 121 cells are switched off, in a perfectly rectangular block
     down the right-hand side.

  Eleven rows, seven usable columns per row. Seven bits is exactly one
  ASCII character, and the four empty columns are the four bits standard
  ASCII does not need. So each row is one letter, and the reason those
  columns are dead is that no printable ASCII character needs bit 7.

  Reading each row LSB first:

  frame 0, 38 stars, columns 7..10 all empty
     . . * . * . * . . . .   0010101 ->  84 -> 'T'
     . . . * . * * . . . .   0001011 -> 104 -> 'h'
     * . * . . * * . . . .   1010011 -> 101 -> 'e'
     . . . . . * . . . . .   0000010 ->  32 -> ' '
     . * * * . * * . . . .   0111011 -> 110 -> 'n'
     * . . * . * * . . . .   1001011 -> 105 -> 'i'
     * * * . . * * . . . .   1110011 -> 103 -> 'g'
     . . . * . * * . . . .   0001011 -> 104 -> 'h'
     . . * . * * * . . . .   0010111 -> 116 -> 't'
     . . . . . * . . . . .   0000010 ->  32 -> ' '
     * * . . * * * . . . .   1100111 -> 115 -> 's'

  frame 1, 38 stars, columns 7..10 all empty
     * * . * . * * . . . .   1101011 -> 107 -> 'k'
     * . . * * * * . . . .   1001111 -> 121 -> 'y'
     . . . . . * . . . . .   0000010 ->  32 -> ' '
     * . . . . * * . . . .   1000011 ->  97 -> 'a'
     * * * . * * * . . . .   1110111 -> 119 -> 'w'
     * . . . . * * . . . .   1000011 ->  97 -> 'a'
     * . . * . * * . . . .   1001011 -> 105 -> 'i'
     . . * . * * * . . . .   0010111 -> 116 -> 't'
     * * . . * * * . . . .   1100111 -> 115 -> 's'
     . . . . . * . . . . .   0000010 ->  32 -> ' '
     . . . . . * . . . . .   0000010 ->  32 -> ' '

  A detail that makes the reading self-checking: every one of the 22 rows
  decodes to a printable character on the first try. Get the bit order
  backwards and you get control codes and garbage, so the fact that all 22
  land in the printable range, and spell English, is the confirmation. The
  two trailing spaces in frame 1 are padding to fill the frame.

  Two consequences beyond the joke, and both mattered.

  First, the frame is 121 bits long. That is the strongest early evidence
  that the input format is a fixed 121-cell frame and not a free-running
  stream, and 121 = 11 x 11 is where the grid comes from. Later the SAT
  solve proved the same thing independently: at 121 clock edges the netlist
  provably cannot produce success, at 122 it can.

  Second, the word "sky", and then "stars" from the output side, said this
  puzzle was about a night sky before I understood a single gate. The file I
  had written off as a dead end on day one had told me the theme on day one.

WHEN
  Day 1, straight after egg 05. Switching the output bus to ASCII made me
  go back and ask the same question about the input bus, and this fell out
  within a few minutes of counting the stars per frame.
